Right where you work

Undercurrent sits in VS Code's bottom panel and status bar. Most of the time you'll forget it's there, which is the point.

How it moves

Four states, all driven by what your hands are doing. Every change is a slow ramp, so the music shifts beneath your attention instead of pulling on it.

In flow

You're typing. The music plays at your chosen level.

0 dB

Thinking

You stopped typing for longer than your usual thinking pause. The music dips to about half as loud over 45 seconds. Scrolling and reading keep it here, never lower.

−9 dB · after ~60 s, learned from you

Away

Five minutes with no typing, scrolling or navigation. The music fades to silence over two minutes and the audio engine sleeps.

silence · after 5 min

Back

Three keystrokes and it returns within seconds, picking up mid-phrase. A stray keypress won't wake it.

3 edits in 15 s · 6 s rise

Built on research, tuned for code

Each default comes from a finding about attention, music or how programmers work. Where the evidence is thin we say so, and every threshold can be changed.

Instrumental only

Speech in the background competes with the verbal side of working memory, an effect strongest for music with lyrics. Code is language, so every track is wordless.

Salamé & Baddeley, 1989

Slow and soft

Fast, loud background music hurt reading comprehension. Slow music at a lower volume did not. Tracks sit between 56 and 66 bpm and default to background level.

Thompson, Schellenberg & Letnic, 2012

Mood is the mechanism

The famous "Mozart effect" turned out to be a lift in arousal and mood, not anything special about Mozart. Software developers who worked with music they chose reported better mood and better quality of work.

Thompson, Schellenberg & Husain, 2001 · Lesiuk, 2005

Changes you don't notice

Sudden changes in sound trigger an automatic orienting response that pulls attention toward them. So nothing in Undercurrent starts, stops or jumps. Dips take 45 seconds and fades take two minutes, ramped evenly in decibels.

Sokolov, 1963 · Näätänen et al., 2007

Thinking is coding

Professional developers spend about 58% of their time understanding code rather than writing it. Reading keeps the music alive, and a pause is judged against your own rhythm, learned from your last few hundred thinking pauses.

Xia et al., 2018 · Medimorec & Risko, 2017

Getting back is the hard part

After an interruption, programmers rarely resume editing within a minute. When you return, the music comes back within seconds and continues from where it was, a familiar cue that you're back in it.

Parnin & Rugaber, 2011

Noise suits some minds

A moderate level of ambient noise helped creative thinking, and steady noise helped inattentive students on memory tasks. If melody is too much for you, brown noise gets the same flow-following behaviour.

Mehta, Zhu & Cheema, 2012 · Söderlund et al., 2007

What the research doesn't say

Across studies, background music's effects on performance are small and differ from person to person. Music won't put you in flow. It can make the conditions more comfortable and mark the moment you begin. That's why Undercurrent adapts to you and never asks for attention.

Kämpfe, Sedlmeier & Renkewitz, 2011 · Csikszentmihalyi, 1990

Running in VS Code in a minute

No terminal, no build. Undercurrent only notices that the file in front of you changed, never what you typed, and nothing leaves your machine.

  1. Download the extension One small file, about 22 KB. Download undercurrent.vsix
  2. Install it in VS Code Open the Command Palette with Ctrl Shift P (Mac: Cmd Shift P), type install from vsix, press Enter, and choose the file you downloaded.
  3. Click once, then type Click Open Player in the welcome message, then click inside the player once to allow sound. Start typing in any file and the music comes in.
Prefer the terminal? code --install-extension undercurrent.vsix Run it in the folder you downloaded to, then restart VS Code.
